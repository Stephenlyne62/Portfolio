The Product

Replacing spreadsheets
with a real application.

World Cup prediction competitions are often managed through spreadsheets, messages and manual score calculations.

I built a dedicated web application to centralise the competition, automate scoring and give participants a single place to submit predictions and follow the standings.

Business Logic

Scoring and prediction integrity

Predictions remain editable before a fixture begins, then lock against the server-side fixture time. Completed results are processed against saved predictions to update competition standings.

Challenges

Key engineering challenges

The most important engineering problems centred on protecting prediction integrity, automating competition logic and keeping tournament-scale data manageable.

01

Prediction locking

Users needed editing access before kick-off while being prevented from changing predictions once a fixture had started.

02

Automated scoring

Match results needed to be compared against saved predictions consistently and reflected accurately in the leaderboard.

03

Tournament scale

Supporting 104 fixtures required reusable components, structured data and manageable filtering across the tournament schedule.

04

Responsive data

Fixtures, standings and prediction controls needed to remain clear and usable across desktop and smaller mobile screens.

Outcome

What World Cup Predictor demonstrates

End-to-end ownership of a complete full-stack application — from product design and responsive interfaces through authentication, database integration, business logic and production deployment.

The application supports a real competition with 104 fixtures and 21 participants, replacing manual prediction tracking and score calculation with a single deployed product.
