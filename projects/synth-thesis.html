Overview

The research question

Traditional software synthesizers often expose controls through technical concepts such as oscillator shapes, envelopes, filters, modulation, and signal routing.

Although these controls provide flexibility, novice users may struggle to connect them with the musical result they want to create.

The project investigates whether presenting equivalent functionality through a more educational and goal-oriented interface can improve usability, confidence, and understanding for novice users.

Ownership

What I developed

I independently designed and developed two software synthesizer interfaces supported by the same underlying sound-generation system.

  • A conventional synthesizer interface based on familiar technical controls
  • An educational interface designed around clearer musical intentions and sound characteristics
  • A shared object-oriented C++ synthesis and DSP engine
  • Oscillator, envelope, filtering, and signal-processing functionality
  • A controlled comparison designed to isolate interface design as the principal variable
  • A user-evaluation process covering usability, confidence, understanding, and task completion

Project

Interfaces and research design

Both interfaces provide access to the same synthesis functionality. The visual and interaction design changes, while the underlying audio engine remains consistent.

Engineering

Technical approach

The synthesizer was structured around a shared object-oriented audio engine. Both interfaces communicate with the same DSP components and expose the same underlying functionality.

Separating the interface layer from the sound-generation system made the comparison more controlled. Differences in participant experience could be examined in relation to the interface design rather than differences in audio capability.

Research

Controlled user evaluation

The user evaluation was designed to compare how novice users interacted with the two interface approaches.

Participants completed equivalent synthesis activities using both interfaces. Their responses could then be compared across usability, confidence, understanding, and their ability to move towards an intended sound.

Equivalent functionality

Both interface conditions used the same synthesis engine so that the comparison focused on interaction design.

Novice perspective

The study focused on how users without extensive synthesis experience understood and approached the controls.

Comparable activities

Participants followed a consistent evaluation process across the two interface conditions.

Mixed evidence

The evaluation considered participant performance alongside their reported experience and confidence.

Challenges

Key project challenges

Separating interface and audio logic

The software needed a clear boundary between presentation, parameter mapping, and real-time audio processing.

Maintaining functional equivalence

Both interfaces needed to provide comparable capability despite presenting controls through different mental models.

Real-time DSP behaviour

Audio parameters had to respond predictably while preserving stable and continuous sound output.

Designing a valid comparison

The evaluation needed to minimise unrelated differences between the two conditions and keep the interface as the central variable.

Outcome

What the project demonstrates

The project demonstrates the ability to design and implement a substantial independent software system combining object-oriented C++, digital signal processing, interface design, and real-time audio programming.

It also demonstrates research planning, controlled comparison, user-centred product thinking, and the ability to evaluate software rather than treating implementation as the final stage of development.
